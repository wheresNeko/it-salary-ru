## 3. Salary repair policy and its impact

Two repairs are applied, and both are recorded rather than silently done.

**(a) The zero sentinel.** The portal encodes "salary not specified" as
the literal value `0` — the free text reads `"от 0"` — instead of leaving
the field null. A sentinel that looks like real data is the most dangerous
kind of defect: `log(0)` is `-inf`, and a model will otherwise train on
0-rouble monthly salaries. These rows are set to missing and flagged
`salary_zero_sentinel`.

**(b) The degenerate range.** The free-text `salary` field is `"от N"` in
every salaried record, so the text never carries an upper bound. Where
`salary_max <= salary_min` the range carries no information; `salary_max`
is set to missing and the row flagged `salary_open_ended` rather than
guessing whether the employer meant a fixed salary or simply omitted the
upper bound. The raw values are retained in the table, so a different
policy can be evaluated later without re-running the harvest.

## 5. A feature that had to be withdrawn: `requirement.experience`

This field is documented as an integer and reads like a year count. It is
neither usable nor what it appears to be. The investigation is written up
because the same trap waits in any dataset whose field semantics are
documented by implication rather than by a schema.

Observed value distribution:

| Value | Rows | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `1` | 7,923 | 40.47% |
| `0` | 6,480 | 33.10% |
| `3` | 3,416 | 17.45% |
| `5` | 636 | 3.25% |
| `6` | 451 | 2.30% |
| `2` | 376 | 1.92% |
| `4` | 193 | 0.99% |
| `10` | 41 | 0.21% |
| `7` | 34 | 0.17% |
| `18` | 4 | 0.02% |
| `20` | 4 | 0.02% |
| `8` | 3 | 0.02% |

If this were a year count the distribution would be implausible on its
face: 40% of adverts would require exactly 1 year(s) and 33% would require 0, with a tail reaching 42.
Cross-checking it against the experience that the free text actually
states settles it:

| Structured value | Adverts whose text states experience | Range stated |
|---|---:|---|
| `0` | 158 | 1 – 35 years |
| `1` | 924 | 1 – 40 years |

Value `0` accompanies adverts asking for up to 35 years of experience, so
`0` cannot mean "no experience required"; value `10` appears on adverts
whose text reads "от 10 лет" while value `18` appears on ones reading
"от 1 года". The code space has no documented scale and no consistent
reading.

**Decision:** the column is retained as an opaque code for audit, renamed
`experience_code_structured` so nothing reads it as years, and **excluded
from the feature set**.

That leaves the free text as the only experience signal, and its coverage
is poor:

| Source | Rows | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| absent | 17,580 | 89.8% |
| mined-from-text | 1,998 | 10.2% |

**Experience is therefore not a usable feature from this source.** That is
a finding, not a setback: it removes a column that would have looked
informative and been meaningless. It is also the kind of thing that is far
cheaper to discover here than in the middle of Stage 3.

(For completeness: 12 rows carry non-numeric text such as `"от 0"` in that field. Arrow refuses to serialise such a mixed column,
which is how the field first drew attention at all.)

## 6. Known limitations carried into Stage 3

1. **The sample is not random.** Within one query the API returns its own
   ordering, and each (keyword, region) cell is capped at 100 records
   because paging is unreliable. Estimates describe this sample, not the
   population of Russian vacancies.
2. **The portal skews towards state-sector and blue-collar roles.** IT is a
   minority of the 522k national vacancies; the model is valid only within
   the population it was fitted on.
3. **`salary_max` is unusable in ~38% of salaried records**, and the
   free-text salary never carries an upper bound. Only `salary_min` is
   modelled as the target.
4. **Experience is not usable.** `requirement.experience` is an
   undocumented code, not years (section 5), and the free text states an
   experience requirement in only a small minority of adverts. Stage 3
   must not lean on experience as an explanatory variable.
5. **Skill extraction is dictionary-based.** A technology absent from
   `textmining.SKILL_PATTERNS` is invisible; the dictionary is a documented,
   reviewable artefact rather than a learned extractor.
6. **Russian free text is inconsistently spelled.** The Cyrillic/Latin
   folding in `textmining.py` covers the confusions actually observed;
   others may exist.
